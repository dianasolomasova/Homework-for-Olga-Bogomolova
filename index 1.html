My friend Sarah got a degree in Marketing five years ago. After university, she had a lot of career opportunities, but she decided to join an international project in Warsaw. She has worked there for over a year now.

Sarah works 9-to-5, but sometimes she works long hours when the project is busy. Her job doesn't let her work from home — she has to be in the office every day. Her office is in a modern building, and getting there is easy: it takes her only 20 minutes by metro.

Sarah is a very sociable person. She says she is more useful when she works with a big team. Her colleagues like her because she is very responsible — she never leaves a task unfinished. But sometimes she can't stop working, even on weekends. She admits: "I find it difficult to relax."

Her job is well-paid, and her employer is friendly. But there is a lot of politics in the company — she has to be careful with some managers. Also, she sometimes doesn't get it when her boss explains new software. But she always asks for help.

At the moment, Sarah is developing her own service for freelance designers. She hopes it will be profitable in the next two years.

Her husband, Tom, works in a bank. People often ask Sarah what her husband does, and she happily answers: he enjoys his job, he has worked there for three years, and he gets on well with his team. But Tom has one problem: he works in a very hot area (his office is on the top floor, and the air conditioning is broken). He also has to follow strict safety measures during fire drills.

Sarah and Tom are happy. Sarah says: "Work alone? No, thanks. I prefer people."
